# Teacher Pack — Handouts 1–4

Covers the Tier 1 handouts in [`../handouts/census-esl-handouts-1-4.html`](../handouts/census-esl-handouts-1-4.html): the four to run **before Census night, Tuesday 11 August 2026**.

Print the handout file double-sided: each handout is two A4 sides, eight pages total.

---

## Before you teach any of it

**Nothing personal is collected.** Every task runs on the fictional Haddad household. Where a learner writes their own name or street in the boxes (Handouts 2 and 3), the page says *keep this and take it home* — it is never handed in, displayed or marked.

**You teach the form, not the census.** When a learner asks what to put for their own situation — a visa question, a shared-care arrangement, a household that doesn't fit the boxes — the answer is **1800 181 227**, or **TIS National 131 450** for an interpreter. Not your best guess. Handout 1's speaking task and every take-home strip rehearse exactly this, so learners can self-serve after the course ends.

**Compulsory *and* confidential, in that order.** Several learners will have arrived from states where a government headcount was a threat. Handout 1 teaches both words in the same vocabulary set deliberately. Don't teach one without the other.

**Levels are not printed on the learner sheets.** Everyone in the room gets the same page and the same topic. Differentiation is in the ⬇ *More support* and ⬆ *More challenge* boxes, which are phrased as choices rather than as levels. The level mapping below is for your planning only.

## The Haddad household

Reused across the whole series, so learners build familiarity rather than meeting new names each week.

**Address:** Unit 4, 12 Graham Avenue, Fairfield NSW 2165

| Person | Age | Relationship | On Census night |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amal Haddad | 41 | Person 1, householder | Present |
| Yusuf Haddad | 44 | Amal's husband | Present |
| Layla Haddad | 12 | Daughter | Present |
| Nadia Haddad | 6 | Daughter | **Away** — slept at her cousin's, on their form |
| Rana Haddad | 68 | Amal's mother | Present |
| Tomas Silva | 29 | Unrelated boarder | Present — night shift, home Wednesday, not on another form |
| Karim Haddad | 25 | Yusuf's cousin | **Away** — on holiday in Lebanon |
| Sara Okafor | 30 | Visitor, friend from Melbourne | Present — one night only |

Seven people usually live there; six were present on Census night; two were away. That's deliberate: the mismatch between *who lives here* and *who slept here* is the single hardest idea on the form, and Handout 4 is built on it. It also lands the household on exactly six present, which is the form's per-person column limit — the hook for Handout 4's extension task.

## Handout 1 — Census night is Tuesday 11 August

**B. Words** — 1 b · 2 f · 3 e · 4 c · 5 h · 6 a · 7 d · 8 g

Give learners two minutes with a phone or a bilingual dictionary for the *My language* column before you take feedback. Learners who share an L1 should compare — the disagreements are where the useful teaching is.

**C. Understand** — 1 T · 2 F · 3 T · 4 T · 5 F · 6 T · 7 T

Item 2 and item 5 are the two that matter. For 2: the form covers everyone in Australia that night, including non-citizens and international visitors; only foreign diplomats are excluded. For 5: the ABS is legally required not to release information in a way likely to identify an individual. Both facts are on page 2 of the form — send learners there rather than asserting it yourself.

**D. Practise** — Tuesday, 11 August 2026. On the calendar, 11 is in the T column, second row. Numbers: 1800 181 227 · 131 450 · 1800 130 250. Say each in the natural Australian grouping — *eighteen hundred, one-eight-one, two-two-seven* — then again digit by digit.

**E. Do it** — ✓ 1, 2, 3, 4 · ✗ 5, 6

Number 4 is the one to discuss. The form's rule: include anyone who lives at the address who returned the next day without being included on another form, and it names shift workers explicitly. Number 5 fails because he never spent the night. Number 6 fails because she is on another form — not because she is a child.

## Handout 2 — How to write your answers

**B. Words** — 1 a · 2 b · 3 c · 4 d · 5 e · 6 f · 7 g · 8 h

*Specify* and *Go to* are the two with the longest reach. *Other (specify)* appears dozens of times across the form; *Go to 19* is a skip instruction, and following a skip is a reading skill many learners have never been taught.

**C. Understand** — Row 1 ✓. Rows 2–5 ✗: small letters; two letters in one box; no space between words; pencil (the form says blue or black pen only).

**D. Practise** — Part 2: draw a line through the second T and write H. Part 3: blue or black pen.

Give the whole class 30 seconds of silent box-writing before feedback on part 1. One letter per 7mm square is a **motor skill**, not a rule — learners who have written in a different script, or who write rarely, need the rehearsal more than the explanation.

**E. Do it** — A M A L / H A D D A D / T O M A S / S I L V A, one letter per box, capitals.

Mark against the three printed criteria only. Do not correct handwriting style, letter shapes, or anything the form doesn't care about.

## Handout 3 — Where do you live?

**B. Words** — 1 b · 2 d · 3 a · 4 f · 5 c · 6 e · 7 g · 8 h

*Dwelling* is worth dwelling on: it is the form's word for a home, it appears in a dozen questions, and almost no learner will have met it.

**C. Understand** — Unit/flat/apartment number · street number · street name · suburb/locality · state/territory · postcode

**D. Practise**
1. NSW New South Wales · VIC Victoria · QLD Queensland · SA South Australia · WA Western Australia · TAS Tasmania · NT Northern Territory · ACT Australian Capital Territory
2. 1 88 · 2 QUEENS PARADE · 3 CLIFTON HILL · 4 VIC · 5 3068
3. Three mistakes: the street number and street name are **swapped**; *graham avenue* is in **small letters**; the state is written out in full instead of the abbreviation **NSW**. (The postcode and suburb are correct — say so, or learners will hunt for a fourth.)
4. Prepare address cards in advance. Invented addresses only — never learners' own. Six or eight cards is plenty; learners swap and repeat.

**E. Do it** — 4 · 12 · GRAHAM AVENUE · FAIRFIELD · NSW · 2165

## Handout 4 — Who is in your house?

**B. Words** — 1 a · 2 c · 3 b · 4 e · 5 d · 6 g · 7 f · 8 h

**C. Understand** — a 3 · b 5 · c 2 · d 4

**D. Practise** — 1 P · 2 P · 3 P · 4 P · 5 A · 6 P · 7 P · 8 A · 9 N

The pair to argue about is 6 and 9. Tomas was physically absent on Tuesday night but counts as present — he lives there, worked a night shift, came home Wednesday, and is on no other form. Rana's brother was physically present on Tuesday evening but doesn't count — he went home and slept somewhere else. **Sleeping, not visiting, is what the form measures.** If only one idea survives this lesson, make it that one.

Number 5 is worth a light touch: Nadia is away because she is on another form, and children in shared-care or overnight-stay arrangements are common in the room. Keep it about the form, not about the family.

**E. Do it**
- **Q2** — mark all six categories. Me (Amal); Spouse/partner (Yusuf); Babies, children and teenagers (Layla); Other adult family members (Rana); Unrelated housemates, flatmates or boarders (Tomas); Visitors or friends (Sara).
- **Q3** — **6**
- **Q5** — **2** (Nadia and Karim)

Extension: a household with eight people completes the Census online, or requests an extra paper form on 1800 130 250 using the Census number from the front of the form. Page 3.

## Running it in one week

Census night is Tuesday 11 August 2026. If you have four sessions, run them in order. If you have two, this pairing works:

**Session 1 (before Census night)** — Handout 1, then Handout 2. What it is and when; how to physically fill boxes. A learner who has only this can attempt the form.

**Session 2 (before Census night)** — Handout 3, then Handout 4. Address, and who to count. These are the two questions people actually get wrong.

If a session lands *after* the 11th, keep the same content and change the framing verb: *did you include…* instead of *will you include…*. The language is identical and the vocabulary is no less useful — the form's ideas (usual address, household, dwelling, present and away) recur on every government form a learner will meet.

If you have a third session, jump to Handout 12 rather than 5 — the interpreter and help-line content is most useful during the census period itself.

## Materials to prepare

- **Handout file printed double-sided**, one per learner per session.
- **Address cards** for Handout 3 part 4 — six to eight invented Australian addresses on cut-up card, one per pair. Vary unit numbers, states and postcodes.
- **Optional:** a real blank Census form or the ABS sample, one per table, for the "find the rule on page 3 / page 6" extension tasks. Learners navigating a full 26-page authentic document is a genuine skill and worth the table space.
- **A blue or black pen for every learner.** Not a detail — Handout 2 fails without one.
