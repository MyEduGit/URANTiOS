## Keys — Handout 5, Going through the form

**B. Words** — 1 b · 2 d · 3 a · 4 e · 5 f · 6 g · 7 c · 8 h

**C. Understand** — a **21** · b **48** · c **61** · d **17** · e **24** · f **60**

Let learners hunt in the real form rather than telling them. Finding a question number in a 26-page document is the point of the task.

The three-questions box is worth slowing down for. *Optional* (Q24, religion) and *Prefer not to answer* (Q9 gender, Q57 sexual orientation) are the form's own words. Teach them as vocabulary and move on — no discussion, no examples from the room. Everything else on the form should be answered, and the honest answer to "but what about my situation?" is the help line.

**D. Practise**

1. Skip instructions — Q17 Australia → **19** · Q21 English only → **23** · Q30 No → **32** · Q38 no job → **50** · Q58 no one away → **60**
2. One box or all — a **1** · b **ALL** (up to four ancestries) · c **ALL** · d **ALL** (more than one method of travel) · e **1**
3. Three mistakes: *tomas* is in **small letters**; the **age does not match the date of birth** (born 1997 → 29, not 45); **Q18 is blank** when it should have his year of arrival, because he wasn't born in Australia. (Q22 *Well* is fine — say so, or they'll hunt for a fourth.)

**E. Do it** — TOMAS / SILVA / 29 / Unrelated flatmate or co-tenant of Person 1 / Other (specify) BRAZIL / 2019 / Yes, other language: PORTUGUESE / Well

## Keys — Handout 6, Getting help, and being ready

**B. Words** — 1 b · 2 a · 3 c · 4 d · 5 e · 6 f · 7 g · 8 h

**C. Understand** — 1 c · 2 d · 3 a · 4 b · 5 e

All five come straight from page 2 and page 26 of the form. Send learners to those pages to check their own answers.

**D. Practise** — no fixed answers. Listen for the three phrases that matter most: *I need an interpreter who speaks ______*, *Could you explain that in easier English?*, and *Let me check that I understood.* Everything else is a bonus.

**E. Do it** — the checklist is the learner's own. Read it aloud together, line by line, and have them tick what is already true. Anything unticked is what they do between Monday night and Tuesday.

## What this does not cover

Handouts 7–12 of the [full plan](../SERIES_PLAN.md) — the work, education, health, transport and housing blocks in depth. They're not lost, they're just not urgent: those are ordinary settlement-English topics that happen to use the Census as their text, and they stay useful all year. Handout 5 gives learners enough navigation to get through those sections of the form next Tuesday, which is what this week is for.

After Census night, if you want to keep going, pick up at Handout 9 (work: job, tasks, industry). It's the one with the most direct employability payoff, and the *occupation / main tasks / industry* distinction is the same one every résumé and job application asks for.
