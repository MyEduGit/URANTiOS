Census night is Tuesday 11 August

Try the exercises first, then check. Mark your own work. Every answer here has a short reason and a longer one — read the short one first.

B. Words

AnswerWhy — simpleWhy — in detail
1 censusbA count of all the people.The Census counts everyone in Australia on one night, so governments know how many people live where, and what they need.
2 householdfEveryone in one home.A household is all the people who live together in one home. One form covers one household.
3 formeThe paper with the questions.This form has 65 questions across 26 pages. You write your answers in the small boxes.
4 includecPut the person on the form.If you include someone, you write their name and answer the questions for them in their own column.
5 visitorhA guest who sleeps here.A visitor does not live here. But if they slept here on Census night, they go on this form, not their own.
6 compulsoryaYou must do it.The Census is required by law — the Census and Statistics Act 1905. Page 2 of the form says so.
7 confidentialdKept secret.The ABS must keep your information secure and cannot release it in a way that is likely to identify you.
8 returngSend it back.Post the paper form in the Reply Paid envelope — or do it online instead and recycle the paper.

C. Understand

AnswerWhy — simpleWhy — in detail
1TRUEThe form says this date.Census night is printed on the front page: Tuesday 11 August 2026.
2FALSEEveryone is counted.The Census counts everyone in Australia, including non-citizens and international visitors. Only foreign diplomats are left out.
3TRUEBabies count too.The front page says ‘including visitors and babies’. Age makes no difference.
4TRUEIt is the law.Page 2 says the Census is compulsory, under the Census and Statistics Act 1905.
5FALSENobody can see your answers.The ABS is legally required not to release information in a way that is likely to identify an individual.
6TRUEYou can use the internet.Go to census.abs.gov.au, then enter the Census number and temporary password from the front of the form.
7TRUEVisitors are included.Anyone who slept in the dwelling on Census night goes on the form, even for one night only.

D. Practise

AnswerWhy — simpleWhy — in detail
1Tuesday, 11 August 2026The day, the date, the year.Say it as one phrase. In Australia the day comes before the month: 11 August, not August 11.
211 is in the T columnSecond row, second column.The calendar columns are M T W T F S S. The first T is Tuesday, the second is Thursday.
31800 181 227 · 131 450 · 1800 130 250Help · interpreter · extra form.Australians say these in groups: eighteen hundred, one-eight-one, two-two-seven. Ask for it digit by digit if you need to.
4No fixed answerYour own words.Aim for a full sentence: Census night is Tuesday the eleventh of August. Ask three different people.

How to write your answers

Try the exercises first, then check. Mark your own work. Every answer here has a short reason and a longer one — read the short one first.

B. Words

AnswerWhy — simpleWhy — in detail
1 capital letterscBig letters.A B C, not a b c. The form asks for capitals on every page because a machine reads the form.
2 boxfOne small square.Each box holds exactly one letter. The squares are about 7mm — practise writing small.
3 markePut a cross in it.The form says ‘mark response boxes’. A cross is what it shows — not a tick, not a circle.
4 mistakeaSomething wrong.Everyone makes them. The form tells you how to fix one, so a mistake is never a reason to start again.
5 a spacebOne empty box.Between two words, leave one box empty. Without it, the machine reads the two words as one.
6 specifyhWrite it yourself.‘Other (specify)’ appears dozens of times. It means your answer is not in the list, so write it in the boxes.
7 leave blankgWrite nothing.Some questions are not for you — for example, questions for people aged 15 or over. Leave them empty.
8 Go to 19dJump forward.This is a skip instruction. It saves you time: those questions do not apply to your answer.

C. Understand

AnswerWhy — simpleWhy — in detail
Row 1✓ RightCapitals, one per box, one space.This is exactly what page 1 asks for. Everything else in the table breaks one of those rules.
small letters✗ WrongMust be CAPITALS.john smith should be JOHN SMITH. The form says ‘Use CAPITAL letters’.
two in one box✗ WrongOne letter only.Two letters in one square cannot be read. Use the next box.
no space✗ WrongLeave one box empty.JOHNSMITH reads as one word. One empty box separates the first name from the family name.
pencil✗ WrongPen only.The form says a blue or black pen. Pencil can smudge or fade, and may not scan.

AnswerWhy — simpleWhy — in detail

3Blue or black penOnly these two colours.Red, green or pencil may not scan. If you only have a pencil, borrow a pen before you start.
4No fixed answerYour own questions.Listen for the question form: Do I write big letters or small letters? rather than a single word.

Try the exercises first, then check. Mark your own work. Every answer here has a short reason and a longer one — read the short one first.

B. Words

AnswerWhy — simpleWhy — in detail
1 street numberbThe number of your house.For example 12, or 3A, or LOT 37 in the country. It goes in its own small box, before the street name.
2 street namedThe name of your street.GRAHAM AVENUE, GEORGE STREET. Write the whole name, including AVENUE, STREET or ROAD.
3 suburb / localityaYour part of the city or town.FAIRFIELD, CLIFTON HILL. ‘Locality’ is the word used for small country places.
4 state / territoryfNSW, VIC, QLD…Australia has six states and two territories. The form wants the short form, in capitals.
5 postcodecFour numbers.For example 2165. Every suburb has one. It goes in the last small box, next to the state.
6 unit / flat / apartmenteYour number in a building.If you live in a block, this is your own number. If you live in a house, leave it blank.
7 dwellinggThe Census word for a home.It means any place people live: a house, a flat, a caravan, a houseboat. It appears in a dozen questions.
8 usual addresshWhere you really live.The form’s rule: the place where you have lived, or intend to live, for six months or more in 2026.

Two rules

PRESENT — the person slept here on Tuesday 11 August. It does not matter if they live here or not.
Also present: a person who lives here, was at work all night, and came home on Wednesday — if they are not on another form.

AWAY — the person usually lives here, but slept somewhere else on Tuesday 11 August.

2 usually lives herecThis is their home.The form’s rule: lived, or intends to live, at this address for six months or more in 2026.

8 shift workerhWorks at night.The form names shift workers because they are the easiest people to forget: they were at work all night.
