# ESL Handout Series — Australian Census 2026

**Source document:** *Census Household Form — Census night is Tuesday 11 August 2026* (SAMPLE), Australian Bureau of Statistics, © Commonwealth of Australia 2026. 26 printed pages, 65 questions.

**Series title:** *Filling In the Form — English for the 2026 Census*

**Deliverable:** 12 classroom handouts + a teacher pack, built from one authentic document.

---

## 1. Why this document is worth a whole series

The Census form is a rare thing in ESL: a **single authentic text every learner in Australia must engage with, on a known date, in the same week**. It also happens to be a near-complete syllabus of adult settlement English:

| The form contains | Which is really a lesson in |
|---|---|
| Address boxes (Q1, Q13–15, Q47) | Australian address format, state abbreviations, postcodes |
| Household grid (Q2–6, Q10) | Family and relationship vocabulary, `there is / there are` |
| Dates of birth, arrival years (Q7, Q18) | Dates, ordinals, `How old…? / When did you…?` |
| Country / language / ancestry (Q17–24) | Nationality words, self-description, past simple |
| Health block (Q25–29) | Body, health, `need help with -ing` |
| Education block (Q30–36) | School levels, qualifications, present perfect |
| Work block (Q37–51) | Job titles vs. duties vs. industry — the core of employability English |
| Transport (Q48) | Prepositions of transport, daily routine |
| Unpaid work (Q53–56) | Time expressions, `In the last two weeks did you…?` |
| Dwelling block (Q60–64) | Housing and tenancy vocabulary, rent periods, money |
| FAQ page (p.2) | Reading for gist, modals of obligation, help-seeking |

The instruction page alone (*"Use CAPITAL letters"*, *"one letter per box"*, *"draw a line through the box"*) teaches a form-filling literacy that transfers to Medicare, Centrelink, tenancy applications and job forms for years afterwards.

## 2. Assumptions (change these and the plan flexes)

1. **Learners:** adult migrants and refugees in Australia — AMEP, TAFE, community college or volunteer tutoring. Mixed L1s.
2. **Level range:** CEFR **pre-A1 to B1** (roughly ACSF Levels 1–3). Every handout is tagged, and each carries a ⬇ *Support* and ⬆ *Extension* variant so a multi-level room can run one topic.
3. **Session length:** 45–60 minutes, so each handout is a standalone lesson; the series can also be cherry-picked.
4. **Delivery:** printed A4, black-and-white photocopy-safe, plus an on-screen version. No assumption of learner devices.
5. **Timing:** the series is written to be reusable, but Handouts 1–3 are time-critical if run before 11 August 2026.

## 3. Non-negotiable design rules

These are the rules that keep the series ethical and safe. They bind every handout.

**R1 — Practise on a fictional household, never a real one.**
All gap-fills, grids and mock forms use invented characters (the *Haddad* household, the *Nguyen* household, *Person 1 = Amal Haddad*). No handout ever asks a learner to write their own name, address, income, health condition, visa status or religion on a piece of paper that is collected, displayed, marked or left in a classroom. Learners may fill in a personal copy they keep and take home — that is the goal — but it is never handed in.

**R2 — Teach the form; do not complete the form.**
The teacher's role is language, not census administration. When a learner asks "so what do I put for my situation?", the answer is the ABS help line (1800 181 227), the ABS website, or TIS National on 131 450 — not the teacher's guess. Handout 12 makes this referral pathway an explicit learning outcome so learners can self-serve.

**R3 — Flag the sensitive blocks and honour the right to skip.**
Q24 (religion) is legally optional and the form says so — teach the word *optional*. Q9 (gender), Q57 (sexual orientation), Q25–29 (disability and health), Q37 (income) and Q52 (defence service) are all areas where a learner may have trauma, fear of authorities, or a community context that makes public discussion unsafe. Those handouts use **third-person, closed practice only** (matching, labelling, listening) and never pair-interview or whole-class share. Every one of them opens with the sentence *"You do not have to talk about yourself in this lesson."*

**R4 — Separate "compulsory" from "frightening".**
Many learners arrive from states where a government headcount is a threat. Handout 1 and Handout 12 explicitly teach the confidentiality frame the form itself provides — *confidential*, *the ABS cannot release information that identifies you*, *Census and Statistics Act 1905* — as vocabulary, alongside *compulsory*. Both facts, in that order.

**R5 — Attribution and copyright.**
ABS material is generally released under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, but **verify the licence on the specific sample form before distributing derivatives**. Every handout footer carries: *Adapted from the Census Household Form, © Commonwealth of Australia 2026, Australian Bureau of Statistics.* Recreate excerpts as clean typeset boxes rather than photocopying the sample page where practical — it prints better and avoids reproducing the "SAMPLE" watermark.

**R6 — Recycle, don't reset.**
Vocabulary from earlier handouts reappears as warmers in later ones (see §6). By Handout 12 a learner should meet almost nothing genuinely new.

## 4. Scope and sequence

Four modules, twelve handouts. Numbers in the *Form* column are the Census question numbers each handout is built from.

### Module A — Getting started (pre-A1 / A1)

| # | Handout | Form | Core language | Skills focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Census night is Tuesday 11 August** | Cover, p.2 FAQ | *census, night, household, everyone, compulsory, confidential, form* | Reading a public notice for gist; dates; numbers 1800 181 227 said aloud |
| **2** | **How to write your answers** | p.1 instructions | *capital letters, one letter per box, leave a space, mark the box, mistake, draw a line* | Handwriting and form-filling mechanics; the single highest-transfer lesson in the series |
| **3** | **Where do you live?** | Q1, Q13 | *street number, street name, suburb, state, postcode, apartment / flat / unit, property name* | Australian address format; NSW VIC QLD SA WA TAS NT ACT; dictating an address |

### Module B — People in the house (A1 / A2)

| # | Handout | Form | Core language | Skills focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **4** | **Who is in your house?** | Q2–Q5, Q58 | *spouse, partner, de facto, housemate, boarder, visitor, baby, away, usually lives* | `There is / There are`; counting people; *stayed the night* vs *usually lives* — the form's trickiest distinction |
| **5** | **Person 1, Person 2, Person 3…** | Q6, Q10, Q59 | *householder, husband, wife, child, stepchild, sibling, grandchild, son-in-law, aunt, unrelated flatmate* | Reading a grid; family tree → grid transfer; possessive `'s` |
| **6** | **Your details** | Q7–Q9, Q11, Q12, Q16–Q20 | *date of birth, age, sex recorded at birth, gender, marital status, citizen, born in, first arrived* | Dates and years; `Where were you born?` `When did you arrive?`; ⚠ R3 applies to Q9 |

### Module C — Your life in Australia (A2 / B1)

| # | Handout | Form | Core language | Skills focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **7** | **Language, ancestry and religion** | Q21–Q24 | *language other than English, at home, most often, ancestry, optional* | The self-assessment scale *very well / well / not well / not at all* — used as a genuine speaking self-rating activity; ⚠ R3 applies to Q24 |
| **8** | **School and qualifications** | Q30–Q36 | *attending, full-time / part-time student, preschool, primary, secondary, TAFE, certificate, diploma, degree, field of study* | Present perfect `Have you completed…?`; mapping overseas qualifications onto Australian levels |
| **9** | **Work: job, tasks, industry** | Q38–Q46 | *employer, own business, occupation, main tasks, industry, goods produced, services provided* | The three-way distinction the ABS asks for — *REGISTERED AGED CARE NURSE* / *NURSING THE AGED* / *AGED CARE SERVICES*. Directly reusable as résumé practice |
| **10** | **Getting to work** | Q47–Q49 | *train, bus, tram, ferry, car as a driver / as a passenger, walked only, worked at home, did not go to work* | Prepositions with transport; `How do you get to…?`; a class survey with a bar chart |
| **11** | **Unpaid work and looking after people** | Q50, Q51, Q53–Q56 | *unpaid, voluntary work, domestic work, looking after, providing care, actively looking for work* | Time expressions *last week / in the last two weeks / in the last twelve months*; naming invisible labour |

### Module D — Your home and your rights (A2 / B1)

| # | Handout | Form | Core language | Skills focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **12** | **Your home, and getting help** | Q60–Q65, p.2 FAQ, p.26 | *owned outright, owned with a mortgage, rented, real estate agent, community housing, bedrooms, per week / per fortnight / per month, interpreter, relay service* | Money and rent periods; ⭐ the help-seeking outcome — *I need an interpreter. Can I call TIS on 131 450?* |

**Teacher pack (not a handout):** answer keys, a two-page mock form for the fictional *Haddad* household, the vocabulary bank, an audio script list for the dictation tasks, and a one-page multi-level running guide.

## 5. What every handout looks like

Fixed six-part shape, so learners recognise the structure by Handout 3 and teachers can build one in about 90 minutes. Full layout spec in [`HANDOUT_TEMPLATE.md`](HANDOUT_TEMPLATE.md).

| Part | Time | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Look** | 3 min | The real form excerpt, reproduced clean. No task yet — just "what can you see?" |
| **B. Words** | 10 min | 8–12 target items. Picture-match at A1, definition-match at A2, collocation at B1. |
| **C. Understand** | 10 min | Comprehension of the excerpt: true/false, matching a person to a box, "which question number?" |
| **D. Practise** | 15 min | Controlled → freer. Gap-fill, then a fictional-household task, then pair speaking. |
| **E. Do it** | 10 min | A realistic mini-task: complete the fictional household's section correctly. |
| **F. Take home** | — | Detachable strip: the target words, and the ABS help numbers (**1800 181 227**, TIS **131 450**). |

**Standard features on every sheet:** level tag in the corner (A1 / A2 / B1); a ⬇ *Support* box (fewer items, first-letter prompts, word bank) and a ⬆ *Extension* box (write your own, explain to a partner, whole-form scavenger hunt); the attribution footer from R5.

## 6. Recycling map

Each handout re-uses earlier language as its warmer, so the series compounds:

- **H3** address boxes → reappear in **H9** (workplace address, Q47) and **H12** (dwelling).
- **H4/H5** relationship words → reappear in **H11** (looking after own child / a child other than own child, Q55).
- **H2** capital-letter mechanics → is the assessment criterion in every later "Do it" section.
- **H6** dates → reappear in **H8** (year completed) and **H11** (time expressions).
- **H1** *compulsory / confidential* → reappear as the reading text in **H12**.
- The *very well / well / not well / not at all* scale from **H7** becomes the series' own self-assessment scale on the back of the final handout.

---

## 7. Assessment

Light, formative, and never a collected personal form (R1).

- **Per handout:** the "Do it" task, self-corrected against a projected key. Criterion: *did the fictional household's section come out correctly and legibly in capitals?*
- **Mid-series (after H6):** the *Haddad* household mock form, pages 1–2 equivalent. Pair task, 20 minutes.
- **End of series:** a **can-do checklist** learners tick themselves, phrased as outcomes rather than grammar —
  *I can write my address in the right boxes. · I can say who lives in my house. · I can say when I came to Australia. · I can describe my job in three ways. · I can ask for an interpreter.*
- **Evidence for AMEP/ACSF reporting:** the mock form and the checklist together map cleanly to ACSF Writing 2–3 (completing a structured form) and Oral Communication 2–3 (giving personal information, asking for assistance). Confirm against your provider's current mapping before claiming it.

## 8. Build order

Census night is **Tuesday 11 August 2026**. If the series is to be run before then, build in this order; if it is being built as a reusable resource, build in series order.

**Tier 1 — before Census night (H1, H2, H3, H4).** These four carry the time-sensitive content: what the date means, how to physically fill boxes, the address, and who to count. A learner who has only these four can attempt the form.

**Tier 2 — the same fortnight (H5, H6, H12).** Household grid, personal details, and the help-seeking pathway. H12 is Tier 2 despite being last in sequence, because the interpreter and help-line information is most useful *during* the census period.

**Tier 3 — the following weeks (H7, H8, H9, H10, H11).** These have the longest independent shelf life — the work, education and transport handouts are ordinary settlement-English lessons that happen to use the Census as their text, and stay useful all year.

Teacher pack is assembled last, from what the handouts actually turn out to need.

## 9. Risks and how the plan handles them

| Risk | Handling |
|---|---|
| A learner treats the class as official census help and mis-files their real form | R2 — every handout routes to 1800 181 227 / TIS 131 450, and the mock form is visibly fictional |
| Distress around health, gender, orientation, income or defence questions | R3 — third-person closed practice only, no pair interviews, explicit opt-out sentence |
| Fear of a government headcount | R4 — confidentiality taught as vocabulary alongside compulsion, in Handouts 1 and 12 |
| Personal data left in a classroom | R1 — nothing personal is ever collected; personal copies go home |
| Copyright on the reproduced form | R5 — verify licence, attribute on every sheet, retypeset rather than photocopy |
| Content dates and becomes wrong for the next census | Question numbers are cited throughout so a future revision can be diffed against the new form rather than rewritten |
| Level mismatch in a mixed room | ⬇/⬆ boxes on every sheet; one topic, three entry points |
