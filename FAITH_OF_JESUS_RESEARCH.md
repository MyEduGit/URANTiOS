# The Faith OF Jesus — Not the Faith IN Jesus

**A research compendium from The Urantia Book (sole authority), with the external
scholarly landscape mapped as context.**

---

## 0. The Emphasis

**This is the one to emphasize: the faith OF Jesus.** Christianity as it stands
is built on faith IN Jesus — the worship of the divine Christ as object. That
faith is not wrong; it is incomplete, and its incompleteness is precisely the
"greatest mistake" the book names (196:2.4). The recovery the book calls for —
and the recovery this whole body of work exists to serve — is the faith Jesus
himself *lived*: his own childlike-but-mighty trust in the Father, which every
mortal can appropriate (101:6.17) and share (196:1.5). Faith IN Jesus looks *at*
him; the faith OF Jesus looks *with* him — at the Father. Everything below is
organized to serve that emphasis.

---

## 1. The Thesis

The Urantia Book draws a sharp, deliberate distinction between two religions that
share one name:

| | The religion **of** Jesus | The religion **about** Jesus |
|---|---|---|
| Founder-experience | The personal religious experience of the Jesus of Galilee | The personal religious experience of the Apostle Paul (196:2.1) |
| Object | The Father, trusted **as Jesus trusted him** | The risen and glorified Christ, worshiped |
| Verb | **Lived** | **Preached** (196:2.1) |
| Faith | The faith **of** Jesus — his own living, saving faith, to be shared | Faith **in** Jesus — belief directed at him as object |
| Gospel | The fatherhood of God and the brotherhood of man | The atoning person and return of the divine Christ |

The book's own formulation is exact:

> "You may *preach* a religion *about* Jesus, but, perforce, you must *live* the
> religion *of* Jesus." — **196:2.1**

> "But the greatest mistake was made in that, while the human Jesus was recognized
> as *having* a religion, the divine Jesus (Christ) almost overnight *became* a
> religion." — **196:2.4**

> "It should not be the aim of kingdom believers literally to imitate the outward
> life of Jesus in the flesh but rather to **share his faith**; to trust God as he
> trusted God and to believe in men as he believed in men." — **196:1.5**

The whole of **Paper 196** carries the title **"The Faith of Jesus"** — the
capstone paper of the entire revelation. The book ends on this note.

> "Great things happened not only because people had faith in Jesus, but also

natures of the Master." The corrective is not the abolition of faith in Jesus as
Sovereign Son, but the recovery of his own faith as the thing to be *lived*. The
distinction is a re-centering, not a negation.

## 3. Synthesis

1. **Faith of Jesus = his own faith-experience**, the actual trust by which the
   human Jesus achieved God-consciousness and sovereignty (196:1.6–13). It is
   transferable: mortals **appropriate** it (101:6.17), **share** it (196:1.3,
   196:1.5), and believe **with** him — *as* he believed (196:0.14) — rather than
   only *in* him.
2. **Faith in Jesus = the post-Pentecost object-faith** that made the divine
   Christ into a religion (196:2.4) — true as far as it goes, but it displaced
   the human Jesus and his gospel of the Father (98:7.11, 196:2.5).
3. The book's program is the **figurative resurrection of the human Jesus**
   (196:1.2): a coming reformation in which the living religion *of* Jesus
   supplants — by fulfilling, not destroying — the theologic religion *about*
   Jesus.
4. The two are reconciled in the person of the Master himself: one religion that
   honors both natures (196:2.6), where faith in the Sovereign Son leads into the
   faith of the Son of Man.

## 4. External Scholarly Landscape (context only — not authority)

Under this repository's covenant, The Urantia Book and its Foreword are the sole
authority. The following is mapped strictly as context, because mainstream New
Testament scholarship independently arrived at the same grammatical fault line.

**The *pistis Christou* debate.** Paul's phrase πίστις Χριστοῦ (Rom 3:22, 3:26;
Gal 2:16, 2:20, 3:22; Phil 3:9; Eph 3:12; cf. Rev 14:12 "the faith of Jesus") is
grammatically ambiguous:

- **Objective genitive** → "faith **in** Christ" (Christ as object of our faith)
  — the reading of most modern translations and, classically, of J. D. G. Dunn.
- **Subjective genitive** → "the faith/faithfulness **of** Christ" (Christ's own
  faith) — the reading of the KJV ("the faith of Jesus Christ," Gal 2:16) and of
  Richard B. Hays, whose 1983 dissertation *The Faith of Jesus Christ: The
  Narrative Substructure of Galatians 3:1–4:11* ignited the modern debate.
  Hays argued the subjective reading recovers the humanity of Jesus, the
  cruciform character of discipleship, and salvation as participation in
  Christ's own faithfulness — followed variously by Douglas Campbell, Michael
  Gorman, and N. T. Wright.
- **Third view** — "Christ-faith" as a qualitative genitive: the kind of faith
  Christ had and creates.

Landmark survey volume: Michael F. Bird & Preston M. Sprinkle (eds.), *The Faith
of Jesus Christ: Exegetical, Biblical, and Theological Studies — The Pistis
Christou Debate* (2009).

**The convergence.** Hays's central theological claims — that Paul's gospel rests
on Jesus' *own* faith, that the humanity of Jesus was eclipsed by the exalted
Christ, and that discipleship means participating in that faith — restate, in
academic idiom and half a century later, what Paper 196 asserted plainly: the
human Jesus *had* a religion; the divine Christ *became* one; salvation is the
appropriation of the faith of Jesus. The Urantia Papers (published 1955) thus
anticipated the post-1983 scholarly turn.
