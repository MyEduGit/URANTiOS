# 03 — TERMINOLOGY AND ONTOLOGY

Eight related but distinct expressions govern this project. Conflating them is
the root error of most discussion in this field; this document fixes their
senses for all deliverables. Urantia citations are TEXTUAL OBSERVATIONS of that
corpus; Greek notes summarize the linguistic layer detailed in
`05_BIBLICAL_PASSAGE_MATRIX.md` and `06_PISTIS_CHRISTOU_DEBATE.md`.

## The eight terms

### 1. The religion OF Jesus
The actual, lived, personal religious experience of Jesus of Nazareth: his
faith, prayer, trust, and consecration to the Father's will. "The religion of
Jesus was wholly based on the living of his bestowal life on earth" (141:7.4).
It is a religion Jesus *had* and *lived* — transmissible only by being lived in
turn: "You may *preach* a religion *about* Jesus, but, perforce, you must
*live* the religion *of* Jesus" (196:2.1).

### 2. Religion ABOUT Jesus
The post-Pentecost religion in which the divine Christ is himself the object:
doctrines, creeds, and worship *concerning* him. "The human Jesus was
recognized as *having* a religion [while] the divine Jesus (Christ) almost
overnight *became* a religion" (196:2.4). Christianity "has more and more
become a *religion about Jesus*" (195:10.15). Note: the Urantia text calls this
transformation "unintended" (195:4.4) and still judges Christianity "the best
existing exponent of his lifework" (cf. 195:10.9, 195:10.12) — ABOUT is
incomplete, not worthless.

### 3. Faith IN Jesus
Trust directed *toward* Jesus as object — for salvation, healing, authority.
Greek: πίστις εἰς Χριστόν / objective-genitive πίστις Χριστοῦ. The Urantia
narrative uses the phrase without censure (spies "confessed faith in Jesus,"
148:9.4; Joseph and Nicodemus "kept their faith in Jesus... secret," 188:1.2)
and even honors it reciprocally: "Great things happened not only because people
had faith in Jesus, but also because Jesus had so much faith in them"
(171:7.8). Faith IN Jesus is a valid entry point; the error is stopping there.

### 4. The faith OF Jesus
Jesus' own faith — the subjective genitive taken experientially: the personal,
living, original, spontaneous, purely spiritual trust that "securely held him"
(196:0.5). It has cosmic standing: it "approached the status of a universe
absolute" (101:6.16) and is transferable — "Through the appropriation of the
faith of Jesus, mortal man can foretaste in time the realities of eternity"
(101:6.17). This is the project's center of gravity. Biblical anchor:
Revelation 14:12 τὴν πίστιν Ἰησοῦ; Hebrews 12:2 (Jesus as ἀρχηγός καὶ
τελειωτής of faith).

### 5. The faithfulness OF Jesus
The *pistis*-as-fidelity reading: Jesus' covenant loyalty and obedience unto
death (Rom 5:19; Phil 2:8). This is the sense most subjective-genitive
scholars (Hays, Campbell, Gorman) actually defend — Christ's faithfulness as
the ground of salvation. Distinct from sense 4: faithfulness emphasizes his
*reliability Godward and manward in action*; faith emphasizes his *inner
trust-experience*. The Urantia corpus contains both: trust (196:0.3) and
unbroken fidelity ("unquestioning loyalty to the Father's will," 196:0.5).

### 6. Believing WHAT Jesus believed
Doctrinal alignment with the content of Jesus' beliefs (fatherhood of God,
sonship of man, the kingdom as the Father's will). Necessary but not
sufficient: content without mode is creed. "One of the most important things in
human living is to find out what Jesus believed" (196:1.3) — the project's
research question 1.

### 7. Believing AS Jesus believed
Adopting the *mode* and *quality* of his believing — childlike but not
childish (196:0.12), fearless, fanaticaless, whole-personality trust. "It
required a strong will and an unfailing confidence to believe what Jesus
believed and *as* he believed" (196:0.14).

### 8. Believing WITH Jesus
The consummation and the project's subtitle. The keystone text:

> "Jesus does not require his disciples to believe in him but rather to believe
> *with* him, believe in the reality of the love of God and in full confidence
> accept the security of the assurance of sonship with the heavenly Father. The
> Master desires that all his followers should fully share his transcendent
> faith. Jesus most touchingly challenged his followers, not only to believe
> *what* he believed, but also to believe *as* he believed. This is the full
> significance of his one supreme requirement, 'Follow me.'" — **196:0.13**

Believing WITH Jesus is co-directional faith: standing beside him, facing the
Father, sharing his trust — WHAT + AS, fused in companionship. Its biblical
cousin is participation (Gal 2:20; John 15:4); its Urantia mechanism is the
indwelling spirit and the Spirit of Truth (194:3.5; 108–110).

## The ontological ladder

The eight terms form an ordered ascent, not a set of rivals:

```
faith IN Jesus                 (entry: trusting the Son)
  → believing WHAT he believed  (content: the Father, sonship, the kingdom)
    → believing AS he believed   (mode: childlike, fearless, whole)
      → the faith OF Jesus        (possession: his own trust, appropriated)
        → believing WITH Jesus     (companionship: co-directional trust)
          → the faithfulness OF Jesus (fruit: loyalty lived out unto the end)
```

The religion OF Jesus is this whole ladder lived; religion ABOUT Jesus is the
ladder's first rung institutionalized as if it were the summit.

## Disambiguation rules for all deliverables

1. Never render πίστις Χριστοῦ as settling this ontology either way; the Greek
   is genuinely ambiguous (see 06) — the ontology is established on wider
   evidence.
2. "Faith of Jesus" in Adventist sources may mean sense 3, 4, 5, or "the
   Christian faith Jesus delivered" (fides quae) — always resolve per source
   (see 09).
3. Urantia claims about what Jesus experienced are URANTIA REVELATORY CLAIMS;
   Gospel claims are TEXTUAL OBSERVATIONS of the Gospels; neither is offered
   as neutral historical proof of the other (standards 8–9).
