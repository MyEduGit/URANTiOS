## 1. History of the debate

1. **Johannes Haussleiter (1891)**, *Der Glaube Jesu Christi und der
   christliche Glaube* (title V) — the modern origin: Paul means Jesus' own
   faith. **Gerhard Kittel (1906)** followed (citation R). **Deissmann**
   proposed the "mystical genitive"/genitive of fellowship (R).
2. **A. G. Hebert (1955)**, "'Faithfulness' and 'Faith'" (V; venue discrepancy
   preserved: attested both in *Theology* 58 and RTR 14) and **T. F. Torrance
   (1957)**, ExpTim 68:111–14 (V): pistis as Hebrew ʼemunah — God's/Christ's
   faithfulness.
3. **James Barr (1961)**, *Semantics of Biblical Language* ch. 7 (V):
   demolished the Hebert–Torrance word-study method; suppressed the
   subjective case for a generation.
4. **George Howard (1967 HTR; 1974 ExpTim)** (V): grammatical/redemptive-
   historical revival.
5. **Hultgren (NovT 22 [1980]: 248–63)** vs. **L. T. Johnson (CBQ 44 [1982]:
   77–90)** (both V): the immediate pre-Hays skirmish.
6. **Richard B. Hays (1983)**, *The Faith of Jesus Christ: The Narrative
   Substructure of Galatians 3:1–4:11* (SBLDS 56; 2nd ed. Eerdmans 2002, with
   the 1991 SBL Hays–Dunn exchange as appendices — V): the tide-turner.
   Explosion follows: Williams (CBQ 49 [1987]: 431–47, V), Hooker (NTS 35
   [1989]: 321–42, V).
7. **SBL 1991: Hays vs. Dunn** — the set-piece debate (appendix fact V).
8. **2009 consolidation**: **Bird & Sprinkle (eds.), The Faith of Jesus
   Christ** (V); Bird & Whitenton, Hippolytus (NTS 55 [2009]: 552–62, V) vs.
   Peterman's response (NTS 57.4 [2011]: 594–603, V); Campbell, *Deliverance
   of God* (R).
9. **2010s–2020s**: Easter's survey (CBR 9.1 [2010]: 33–47, V); Kugler's
   survey (CBR 14.2 [2016]: 244–55, V); Morgan, *Roman Faith and Christian
   Faith* (2015, V); Schliesser's third view (JSNT 38.3 [2016], V); Bates,
   *Salvation by Allegiance Alone* (2017, V); Downs & Lappenga, *The
   Faithfulness of the Risen Christ* (title V); **NRSVue (2021) prints "the
   faith of Jesus Christ"** in Rom 3:22 / Gal 2:16 / 2:20 / 3:22 (V
   descriptive); van Driel, *Rethinking Paul* ch. 3 "Jesus' Faith" (CUP 2021,
   V). 2024 survey articles show the debate live (titles V).

## 3. The argument bundles (steelmanned)

### A. Objective genitive — "faith in Christ"
1. πίστις + genitive can be objective (Mark 11:22 πίστιν θεοῦ; Acts 3:16) [R].
2. Hultgren: the disputed formulae are anarthrous; Paul's clear subjectives
   are articular (Rom 3:3 τὴν πίστιν τοῦ θεοῦ) [V citation].
3. Paul never puts Christ as subject of πιστεύειν; no unambiguous "Jesus
   believed" anywhere in Paul [R — Dunn's signature point].
4. Context: both Rom 3:22 and Gal 2:16 pair the phrase with explicit human
   believing; the genitive names faith's object; repetition is emphasis [R].
5. Reception: the Greek fathers, native speakers, read it objectively
   (Harrisville; Peterman's Hippolytus rebuttal V) — countered by Wallis's
   broader early-tradition evidence (R).
6. Hab 2:4 read anthropologically anchors ἐκ πίστεως in believers [R].

### B. Subjective genitive — "the faith/faithfulness of Christ"
1. Every other Pauline πίστις + genitive-of-person is subjective: Rom 3:3
   (God's), Rom 4:12,16 (Abraham's — τῷ ἐκ πίστεως Ἀβραάμ structurally
   parallel to Rom 3:26 τὸν ἐκ πίστεως Ἰησοῦ) [R staple].
2. Redundancy: objective renderings make Rom 3:22/Gal 2:16 tautologous
   ("faith in Christ... to all who believe"); subjective gives each clause
   distinct work [R].
3. Hays's narrative case: Gal 1:4 and 2:20b ("who loved me and gave himself
   for me") epexegete the Son's πίστις; Rom 5:15–19 (the one man's obedience)
   is the parallel soteriology [V thesis].
4. Theological: salvation rests on Christ's act, not on the quality of human
   believing; van Driel (V): on SUBJ "the exegetical basis for the
   traditional Protestant understanding of human faith as the instrumental
   cause of salvation collapses."
5. Early-reception counterevidence: Hippolytus (Bird–Whitenton V), the
   versional/KJV "faith of" tradition, Wallis's pre-Nicene corpus (R).
6. Gal 3:23–25 "before faith came": πίστις arrives with Christ [V].

### C. Third view — qualitative / event
- Sprinkle (V): πίστις Χριστοῦ as eschatological event — the faith that
  "came" and "was revealed."
- Schliesser (V): qualitative genitive, "Christ-faith" — the Christ-kind of
  faith; exploits "the intrinsic complexity of the Greek genitive."
- Hall (V exists): linguistic case for a compound-like "Christ-faith."
- Hultgren's "Christic faith" anticipates this from the objective side [R].

### D. Relational / allegiance reframing
- Morgan (V-partial): Greco-Roman pistis/fides is an enacted, two-directional
  trust-bond; the genitive names the trust-relationship Christ mediates; the
  subj/obj alternative is a false dilemma.
- Bates (V): pistis in salvation contexts = allegiance — mental affirmation +
  professed fealty + embodied loyalty. Critique: Timmins, JETS 61.3 (2018):
  595–615 (V).

## 4. Special cases

- **Rev 14:12** (see 05 §2, 09): articular τὴν πίστιν + τηρέω ("keep") suits
  a deposit/fidelity reading; four live options (objective; subjective;
  fides quae; NIV's loyalty rendering). Rev 2:13 τὴν πίστιν μου ("my faith,"
  Jesus speaking) is the nearest internal parallel (R).
- **Heb 12:2**: the clearest NT text of Jesus himself exercising πίστις —
  ἀρχηγός καὶ τελειωτής with no "our"; the Heb 11 catalogue climaxes in him
  (exemplarist context 12:1–3); Hebrews never uses the πίστις Χριστοῦ formula
  (TEXTUAL OBSERVATION) yet gives the theme its strongest narrative footing.
- **Rom 1:17 / Hab 2:4 text-forms**: MT "his faith," LXX "my faith" — the
  fork that lets the citation be read of the believer, of God, or of the
  Messiah (R; collation next session).

## 5. Findings for this project (no manufactured consensus)

1. TEXTUAL OBSERVATION: the Greek is formally ambiguous at every occurrence;
   verified constructions underdetermine the choice.
2. FACT (translation history): the rendering has oscillated — Douay/KJV "of"
   → 20th-c. "in" consensus → NET "faithfulness of" (1996–2005) → NRSVue
   "faith of" (2021). The KJV tradition Adventism preserved (09) has
   re-entered the mainstream.
3. SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION (Easter, V): priors about Paul's gospel, not
   grammar, decide the debate — "the true locus" is the construal of the
   gospel itself.
4. INFERENCE for the project thesis: the debate's three live camps each
   secure a piece of the ladder in 03 — objective (faith IN), subjective
   (faith/faithfulness OF), third-view/relational (the Christ-faith one
   enters and shares, closest to "believing WITH"). The project does not
   need to win the genitive; it needs what is uncontested: Heb 12:2 presents
   Jesus as faith's pioneer-perfecter, Rev 2:13 has Jesus say "my faith,"
   and a substantial, growing scholarly tradition — now embodied in two
   major translations — reads Paul as grounding salvation in Jesus' own
   faith(fulness). That is sufficient warrant to pose research questions 1–6
   inside biblical scholarship's own terms.
5. Counter-position preserved (Standards 7, 11): Dunn–Hultgren–Matlock–
   Harrisville remains a fully respectable reading; if it is right, the NT's
   explicit faith-OF-Jesus material reduces to Hebrews, Revelation, and the
   Gospels' portrait — which, note, is still enough to sustain the theme
   (as Easter's Hebrews monograph and Wallis's early-tradition study argue).
