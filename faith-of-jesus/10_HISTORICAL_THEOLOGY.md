## 1. The liberal school: religion OF Jesus vs. religion ABOUT Jesus

- **Adolf von Harnack**, *What Is Christianity?* (Berlin lectures 1899–1900;
  Eng. tr. Saunders 1901). VERIFIED-VIA-SEARCH (CCEL text): "The Gospel, as
  Jesus proclaimed it, has to do with the Father only and not with the Son.
  This is no paradox... but the simple expression of the actual fact as the
  evangelists give it." And: "In the combination of these ideas — God the
  Father, Providence, the position of men as God's children, the infinite
  value of the human soul — the whole gospel is expressed." SCHOLARLY
  INTERPRETATION: dogma as the "acute Hellenization" of Christianity; the
  essence is Jesus' own gospel — the paradigmatic religion-OF-Jesus position.
- **Wilhelm Bousset**, *Kyrios Christos* (1913) — research question 8's first
  pole. SCHOLARLY INTERPRETATION: cultic veneration of Jesus as Kyrios arose
  in Hellenistic Gentile Christianity, not the primitive Palestinian
  community — the religion ABOUT Jesus as post-Jesus development.
- **Larry Hurtado** (*Lord Jesus Christ*, 2003; *How on Earth Did Jesus Become
  a God?*, 2005) — the counter-pole: devotion to Jesus was early, Jewish, and
  explosive, not a late pagan accretion; the Baylor reissue of Bousset carries
  Hurtado's critical introduction. **Disagreement preserved** (Standard 7):
  Bousset vs. Hurtado is unresolved in guild terms; note that even Hurtado's
  early-devotion thesis concerns worship OF Jesus and does not touch the
  question whether Jesus' own faith is shareable.
- **Coinage of the slogan**: no verified single coiner. Wellhausen's adjacent
  dictum "Jesus was not a Christian, but a Jew" is verified (Studia Theologica
  45/2, 1991, on the dictum). The Jefferson attribution ("a religion about
  Jesus, rather than the religion of Jesus") is UNVERIFIED-but-widely-repeated;
  19th-c. Unitarians used the antithesis self-descriptively. INFERENCE: a
  commonplace of liberal Protestantism given scholarly ballast by Harnack/
  Bousset and moral force by Thurman — with its most systematic statement, on
  this project's evidence, in Urantia Papers 195–196.

## 2. The quest, Kähler, and the Abba debate

- **Martin Kähler** (1892): the historisch/geschichtlich distinction — origin
  of "Jesus of history / Christ of faith." (Famous line "the real Christ is
  the preached Christ": UNVERIFIED verbatim.)
- **Albert Schweitzer**, *Quest* (1906), closing lines VERIFIED-VIA-SEARCH:
  "He comes to us as One unknown... He speaks to us the same word: 'Follow
  thou me!'... and, as an ineffable mystery, they shall learn in their own
  experience Who He is." TEXTUAL OBSERVATION: Schweitzer's own resolution of
  the failed quest is discipleship-experiential — knowing Jesus by following
  in his fellowship — structurally a believing-WITH move.
- **Joachim Jeremias**, *The Prayers of Jesus* (1967): VERIFIED-VIA-SEARCH:
  "there is no analogy at all in the whole literature of Jewish prayer for God
  being addressed as Abba..." — the classic case for Jesus' distinctive filial
  address. **Pushback**: James Barr, "'Abbā Isn't 'Daddy'," JTS 39/1 (1988)
  28–47 (VERIFIED at Oxford Academic): abba was "a more solemn, responsible,
  adult address to a Father" (search-mediated quote). Current consensus:
  uniqueness weakened; the datum of Jesus' characteristic abba-address (Mark
  14:36; Rom 8:15; Gal 4:6) stands.

## 3. Bultmann: the strongest counter-position

VERIFIED-VIA-SEARCH (*Theology of the NT* I): "The message of Jesus is a
presupposition for the theology of the New Testament rather than a part of
that theology itself," and "he who formerly had been the bearer of the message
was drawn into it and became its essential content. **The proclaimer became
the proclaimed.**" For Bultmann, faith exists only as response to the kerygma;
Jesus' own religion is inaccessible and theologically beside the point.
**Steelman standing (Standard 12):** this is the ablest modern defense of a
faith-IN-only Christianity; any faith-OF program must answer it. The
post-Bultmannian reopening — Käsemann's new quest; **Gerhard Ebeling, "Jesus
and Faith" (in *Word and Faith*)**, the essay that made Jesus' own faith a
live theological topic again — is the historical hinge (Ebeling text
UNVERIFIED this session; see 12).

## 4. Barth and Bonhoeffer

- **Barth**, CD IV/1 §59 "The Obedience of the Son of God": VERIFIED-VIA-SEARCH
  thesis: "...the eternal Son of the eternal Father became obedient by
  offering and humbling Himself"; "in relation to God... this man wills only
  to be obedient." Obedience is relocated into the divine life; Barth speaks
  of Jesus' faithfulness rather than making him a believer among believers.
- **Bonhoeffer**, *Discipleship* (1937): VERIFIED-VIA-SEARCH: "Only he who
  believes is obedient, and only he who is obedient believes." Faith fused to
  following — a Protestant carrier of the discipleship emphasis.

## 5. Thurman: the liberative religion of Jesus

**Howard Thurman**, *Jesus and the Disinherited* (1949). VERIFIED-VIA-SEARCH
(opening chapter): "The basic fact is that Christianity as it was born in the
mind of this Jewish teacher and thinker appears as a technique of survival for
the oppressed." The JSHJ article "Howard Thurman and the Religion of Jesus"
(17/3, 2019) confirms Thurman "articulates the significance of the religion of
Jesus, versus religion about Jesus, for the disinherited." Thurman is the one
canonical modern author for whom "the religion of Jesus" is an explicit,
load-bearing category — bridging the critical distinction into lived,
liberative spirituality. (MLK "carried it in his briefcase": UNVERIFIED
legend.)

## 6. The Catholic question: did Christ have faith? (research question 5)

- **Aquinas, ST III q.7 a.3 — NO.** VERIFIED-VIA-SEARCH (newadvent text):
  "from the first moment of His conception Christ saw God's Essence fully.
  Hence there could be no faith in Him"; faith "implies a certain defect with
  regard to that matter; and this defect was not in Christ." The
  beatific-vision tradition makes faith formally impossible in Christ.
  **Steelman standing (Standard 12):** the classical denial at full strength.
- **The modern re-opening:** Hans Urs von Balthasar, "Fides Christi" (1961, in
  *Sponsa Verbi* / *Explorations in Theology* II): Christ's existence as
  "total and comprehensive openness towards God the Father" — archetypal
  faith inclusive of ours (characterization via secondary essay; Balthasar
  verbatim UNVERIFIED; traditionalist Catholic pushback exists and is
  preserved). **Gerald O'Collins & Daniel Kendall, "The Faith of Jesus,"
  Theological Studies 53 (1992)** (VERIFIED existence; PDF located): notes
  the tradition "did not normally raise" the question, and that this shaped
  translations — e.g., RSV's Heb 12:2 "pioneer and perfecter of OUR faith,"
  where "our" has no Greek warrant. **Jacques Guillet, *The Consciousness of
  Jesus*** — catalogued, UNVERIFIED content. **ITC 1985**, "The Consciousness
  of Christ Concerning Himself and His Mission": defends Jesus' consciousness
  of sonship/mission without using "faith" for Jesus (propositions UNVERIFIED
  verbatim).
- **Pope Francis, *Lumen Fidei* ¶18 (2013)** — VERIFIED-VIA-SEARCH
  (vatican.va): "In faith, Christ is not simply the one in whom we believe...
  he is also the one with whom we are united precisely in order to believe.
  **Faith does not merely gaze at Jesus, but sees things as Jesus himself sees
  them, with his own eyes: it is a participation in his way of seeing.**"
  TEXTUAL OBSERVATION: a magisterial-level formulation materially equivalent
  to "believing WITH Jesus" — the single most striking convergence found in
  the historical-theology corpus.

## 7. Orthodoxy, imitation, participation

- **Athanasius**, De Inc. 54: "He became man that we might become God"
  (standard translation; Greek UNVERIFIED). **Lossky** (VERIFIED-VIA-SEARCH):
  "The Son has become like us by the incarnation; we become like Him by
  deification, by partaking of the divinity in the Holy Spirit." Theosis =
  participation, expressly contrasted by Orthodox writers with external
  imitation.
- **Thomas à Kempis**, *Imitation of Christ* I.1 (VERIFIED-VIA-SEARCH, CCEL):
  "By these words of Christ we are advised to imitate His life and habits..."
- INFERENCE: the imitation/participation polarity maps onto believing-LIKE
  Jesus vs. believing-WITH Jesus; Orthodoxy presses past the model to the
  union. The Urantia corpus makes the same move explicitly: "not... literally
  to imitate the outward life... but rather to share his faith" (196:1.5).

## 8. Quaker and Anabaptist carriers

- **George Fox / Quakers**: "Christ has come to teach his people himself" —
  the characteristic Fox message (reported form across Quaker sources; exact
  Journal locus UNVERIFIED). Institutionalized immediacy: the living Christ
  teaching inwardly now.
- **Hans Denck** (Anabaptist, c. 1495–1527): motto confirmed via GAMEO: "No
  one may truly know Christ except one who follows Him in life" (German
  original and precise locus UNVERIFIED; commonly assigned to *Was geredet
  sei*, 1526). Nachfolge against merely forensic faith — the classic radical-
  Protestant carrier of the faith-OF-Jesus-as-followed-life.

## 9. Modern historical-Jesus research on Jesus' own religious experience

- **James D. G. Dunn**, *Jesus and the Spirit* (1975): the standard study —
  Jesus' experience of God as sonship-consciousness and Spirit-consciousness,
  the relation "out of which His other basic convictions... arose"
  (review-mediated quote).
- **Geza Vermes**: *The Religion of Jesus the Jew* (1993) — the title itself
  a religion-OF-Jesus statement; Jesus as Galilean charismatic hasid in the
  line of Honi and Hanina ben Dosa.
- **Marcus Borg**: Jesus as "spirit person," for whom God "was a reality that
  was experienced" (VERIFIED-VIA-SEARCH).
- **N. T. Wright**, *JVG* (1996): VERIFIED-VIA-SEARCH (secondary-mediated):
  Jesus' self-knowledge was "like knowing one is loved"; his vocation was
  "grasped in faith, sustained in prayer, tested in confrontation, agonized
  over in further prayer and doubt"; "Jesus knew he could have been making a
  terrible mistake." TEXTUAL OBSERVATION: mainstream historical scholarship
  explicitly using "faith" for Jesus' own epistemic stance.
- **Dale Allison**: apocalyptic-prophet framing; limits of reconstruction
  (no verbatim quotes obtained).
- **Schweitzer, *Psychiatric Study of Jesus*** (1913): refuted the
  pathographies; defended Jesus' sanity — relevant to research question 9's
  psychological reconstruction (the balanced-personality datum agrees with
  100:7 and 196:0.7, noted as convergence, not proof).

## 10. Synthesis for the ledger (INFERENCE)

Across twenty centuries the faith-OF-Jesus emphasis persistently re-emerges —
Denck, Fox, Waggoner (see 09), Harnack, Thurman, Ebeling, Balthasar,
O'Collins, Hays (see 06), Lumen Fidei — and is persistently opposed from two
directions: the beatific-vision denial (Aquinas: Christ *could not* have
faith) and the kerygmatic denial (Bultmann: Christ's faith is inaccessible
and beside the point). The project's thesis must hold, and on this evidence
can hold, the narrow ridge between them: Jesus' faith as real, human,
developmental (contra Aquinas's classical form), and as knowable and
shareable (contra Bultmann) — the position the Urantia corpus states at
maximal explicitness (196:0.13; 196:1.5–6; 101:6.17) and Lumen Fidei ¶18
states at magisterial register.
